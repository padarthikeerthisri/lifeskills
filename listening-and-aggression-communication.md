# Listening and Active Communication

## 1. Active Listening

### Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening? (Minimum 6 points)

1. **Give your full attention.** Put away your phone, close extra tabs, and face the speaker so they know you are really listening.
2. **Do not interrupt.** Let the other person finish talking before you start thinking about what to say next.
3. **Use friendly body language.** Nod your head, make eye contact, and sit up straight to show you are interested.
4. **Pay attention to feelings.** Listen to the words, but also notice the person's tone of voice and facial expressions to see how they feel.
5. **Ask questions to understand better.** If something is not clear, ask simple questions like, "Can you tell me more about that?" or "What do you mean?"
6. **Repeat the main idea in your own words.** Check if you understood correctly by saying something like: "So, you mean the deadline is hard because we got the details late. Is that right?"
7. **Do not judge too quickly.** Keep an open mind and listen to the whole story before you give advice or disagree.
8. **Take your time before you answer.** Once you fully understand, share your thoughts in a calm and respectful way.

Active listening means focusing completely on the speaker, understanding their feelings, and making sure you really get their message.

## 2. Reflective Listening

### Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

Reflective listening means listening carefully and then repeating what the person said using your own words. This helps the speaker feel heard and helps you make sure you did not misunderstand them.

The main points are:

* **Focus fully:** Listen carefully without looking at distractions or cutting the person off.
* **Understand the whole message:** Try to catch both the facts and the feelings behind the words.
* **Say it in your own words:** Tell them what you heard, like: "It sounds like you are worried because the deadline is very close. Is that correct?"
* **Check your understanding:** Ask the speaker if you got it right or if you missed anything.
* **Show care and respect:** Accept their feelings without judging them or saying they are wrong.
* **Let them fix your mistakes:** If you misunderstood, let the speaker correct you and then listen again.
* **Finish the communication loop:** This stops misunderstandings because both people know they are talking about the same thing.

Reflective listening is not just repeating words like a parrot. It is about understanding the person's real meaning and checking to be sure you are right.

## 3. Reflection

### Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

Some common things that stop me from listening well are:

* Getting distracted by phone notifications or my own thoughts.
* Planning my answer in my head while the other person is still talking.
* Thinking I already know the answer, so I stop listening carefully.
* Losing focus when explanations are too long or have too many technical words.
* Interrupting people because I am excited to share my own opinion.
* Having no patience to listen when I am tired, stressed, or upset.
* Guessing what the person means instead of asking them to explain.
* Trying to do two things at once in a noisy room.

### Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

To become a better listener, I can:

* Silence my phone and stop multitasking when someone is talking to me.
* Make eye contact and use open body language to show I care.
* Wait until the speaker completely finishes before I start talking.
* Write down notes during long or important meetings.
* Ask questions the moment I feel confused.
* Summarize the main points out loud to make sure we agree.
* Stay calm and patient, even during difficult conversations.
* Wait until the very end before I make up my mind about the topic.
* Practice these steps every day with my friends, family, and coworkers.

## 4. Types of Communication

### Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I use passive communication when I want to avoid arguments or when I do not feel confident. For example, if a teammate gives me extra work, I might say "yes" even if I am already too busy, just because I do not want to disappoint them.

I also become passive around bosses, teachers, or clients because I feel nervous. In these moments, I keep my true thoughts to myself, avoid saying "no," and let others make all the choices for me.

### Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

I change to an aggressive style when I feel angry, stressed, or pushed into a corner. If someone keeps interrupting me or ignores my requests, I might raise my voice, use mean words, or blame them directly.

This also happens when a deadline is very close or when a situation feels unfair. When I am aggressive, I only care about my own point of view and forget to respect the other person's feelings.

### Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment and others) communication styles in your day-to-day life?

I become passive-aggressive when I am upset but do not want to speak up directly. Instead of saying, "I am unhappy about this," I might make sarcastic jokes, complain to other people, or ignore the person completely.

For example, if a friend cancels our plans at the last minute, I might say, "Oh, of course your other plans are more important," instead of calmly telling them that my feelings were hurt. I use these indirect hints because I want to avoid an awkward conversation.

### Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?

Assertive communication means speaking honestly about your needs and feelings while still respecting the other person. You are direct and clear, but you do not blame, insult, or ignore others.

Steps I can take in my life:

* **Use "I" statements:** Instead of saying, "You never listen to me," say, "I feel hurt when I am interrupted. I would like to finish my point."
* **Be clear and specific:** Say exactly what you need. For example: "Please send me the updated file by 10:00 AM tomorrow."
* **Stay calm:** Speak in a steady, normal voice and do not shout.
* **Use confident body language:** Stand or sit straight, look at the person, and do not cross your arms.
* **Say no politely:** If you are too busy, say: "I cannot take on this task right now because I have two deadlines today. Can we find another choice?"
* **Do not exaggerate:** Avoid words like "always" or "never." Instead of saying, "You always make mistakes," say, "This report is missing two important pages."
* **Listen to the other side:** Assertiveness goes both ways. Listen to their ideas so you can find a solution together.
* **Practice with small things:** Start by politely disagreeing or asking for help with simple things, then use these skills in harder situations later.

Assertive communication uses clear requests, honest feelings, confident body language, and "I" statements instead of demands or blame.
